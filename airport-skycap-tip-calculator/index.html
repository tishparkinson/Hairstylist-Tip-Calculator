Your Skycap Tip Suggestions

How much to tip a skycap?

The standard airport skycap tip is $2–$3 per bag for curbside bag check. At high-cost cities or for especially fast and helpful service, $3–$5 per bag is appropriate. Always tip in cash — skycaps do not receive a share of credit card tips.

Are skycaps the same as hotel bellhops?

No. Skycaps work at airports handling curbside check-in and baggage. Bellhops work at hotels moving luggage to your room. Both depend on tips but have different tipping norms — hotel bellhops typically receive $2–$5 per bag, while skycaps are $2–$3 per bag with a higher floor for wheelchair service.

Should you tip before or after?

Tip after — once the skycap has tagged your bags and handed you the claim receipts. Have cash ready before you arrive at the curb so there is no fumbling.

Airport Skycap Tip — What You Need to Know

Skycaps are curbside airline workers who handle your bags at check-in outside the terminal. They are separate from airline employees and depend on tips as a primary source of income.

Cash Only

Always tip in cash. Skycaps do not receive a cut of credit card charges. Have small bills ready — $1s and $5s — before you reach the curb. Asking a skycap to make change creates an awkward moment.

Separate from the Service Fee

Airlines charge a curbside check-in fee ($3–$7) that goes to the airline, not the skycap. The tip is entirely separate and should be given directly to the worker who handled your bags.

Wheelchair Assistance

Tip wheelchair attendants $5–$10 for short escort service and $10–$20 for extended assistance across the terminal or between gates. These workers are among the lowest-compensated in the airport.

When to Tip More

Tip above the standard rate if you have heavy, oversized, or awkwardly shaped bags; if the skycap resolved a check-in problem; or if they moved quickly during a tight connection.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much to tip a skycap?

$2–$3 per bag is the standard airport skycap tip for curbside bag check. For excellent, fast, or heavy-bag service, $3–$5 per bag is appropriate. Always tip in cash directly to the skycap.

What is an airport skycap tip?

An airport skycap tip is a cash gratuity given to the curbside worker who checks your bags outside the terminal. Skycaps are contracted workers — not airline employees — and tips are their primary income supplement.

Do skycaps get to keep their tips?

Yes. Cash tips go directly to the individual skycap or are pooled among the crew on that shift. Either way, the money stays with the skycap workers — unlike credit card tips, which may not reach them at all.

Should you tip a skycap for wheelchair assistance?

Absolutely — $5–$10 for short distances and $10–$20 for longer terminal escorts. Wheelchair attendants are among the most undercompensated airport workers and rely heavily on tips.

Is there a minimum tip for a skycap?

$2 per bag is the widely accepted minimum, with $5 total as a floor even for one light bag. Tipping nothing or less than $1 per bag is considered poor form.

Do you tip a skycap before or after they take your bags?

After — once your bags are tagged and you have the claim receipts. Have cash ready in your coat pocket or a front pocket before you reach the curb so the handoff is natural.

What if I don't have cash for a skycap tip?

Curbside check-in is optional. If you have no cash, check your bags at the airline counter inside instead. There is no obligation to use the curbside service, and skycaps understand — it is far better than being tipped nothing at the curb.
